		\section{Neural Networks}
			A key feature of neural networks is classification based on input variables. Classically, we use linear and nonlinear combinations of \emph{physics} variables to get useful quantities, like using differences of pseudorapidity and \(\phi\) to get a more robust separation:
			\begin{align}
			      \Delta R(\ell_1,\ell_2) 
				  &= \sqrt{(\Delta \eta_{\ell_1,\ell_2})^2 + (\Delta \phi_{\ell_1,\ell_2})^2} 
				  \\
				  m_T
				  &=\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2(E_T^1E_T^2-p^1_T\cdot p_T^2)}
			\end{align}
			\(\Delta R \) and \(m_T\) are useful for identifying certain particles at certain energies, they're used as selection criteria for events. With a neural network, many more variables can be used, typically with better selections. It takes in 5-20 vairables and gives a 1 dimensional score that can quantify signal or background.
